Method

Every day, our scanner pulls fresh DexScreener-listed tokens (new launches and boosted listings) and runs them through independent data sources — GoPlus and honeypot.is for EVM chains, direct Solana RPC plus RugCheck for Solana — checking mint/freeze authority, owner privileges, honeypot behavior, LP lock status, taxes, and holder concentration.

This report covers two layers: 2,448 launches screened across 20 daily runs (Sep 14 – Oct 3, 2026), and a deep-dive snapshot of 40 fresh launches with full per-token checks (37 Solana, 2 Ethereum, 1 BSC).

This is a snapshot review, not an audit. We report what the data shows at scan time; on-chain state can change at any moment. Individual token names are deliberately omitted — the value is in the patterns.

What we actually found

The rug-history cluster is the sharpest signal we have

Three tokens in the sample traced to creators with a documented history of rugged tokens — and all three carried the highest risk scores in the entire batch, two of them also flagged as copycats. If you look at one thing before buying a fresh launch, look at who deployed it and what else they've deployed.

Concentration is the quiet risk: nearly half of launches

45.9% of sampled Solana tokens have a largest holder above 50% of supply (one reached 96.3%). Important caveat: this metric is unadjusted for LP pools, bonding curves, and locked accounts — most of these tokens still score low-risk on supplementary checks. Treat it as an exposure flag worth understanding, not automatic proof of malice. The question that matters: does anyone outside the team control the float?

The good news: Solana launch hygiene is improving

Every single Solana token in the sample (37/37) had both mint and freeze authority renounced. A year ago that number would have been embarrassing. Pump.fun-era defaults made renouncement the norm — one of the genuinely positive shifts in this ecosystem.

EVM launches tell a different story

Of the three EVM tokens deeper-checked, one had an active mint function (supply can be inflated at will) and two showed unlocked, unburned liquidity. Small sample, but the pattern matches what EVM traders know: authority hygiene is worse off-Solana.

Caveats & honesty

  • Snapshot, not audit. Per-token raw data is preserved; we publish aggregates, not accusations against named tokens.
  • The deep-dive sample skewed Solana-heavy (37/40) because the fresh-launch feeds were Solana-dominated at fetch time. EVM findings are indicative at n=3.
  • Holder concentration is unadjusted for LP/bonding-curve/locked accounts — a high number is a question to ask, not a verdict.
  • Honeypot simulation returned no data for the brand-new EVM tokens (a known coverage gap for fresh contracts); absence of a honeypot flag on day-one EVM tokens is weak evidence, and we say so.
  • This report is informational. Do your own research; nothing here is financial advice.
